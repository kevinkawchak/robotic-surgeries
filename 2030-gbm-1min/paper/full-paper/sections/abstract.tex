On-premises repository based large language models (LLMs) provide
commands to standard oncology surgical robots based on real-time
sensor data and controlled via x, y, z coordinates to administer
patient treatment, and this workflow minimizes single robot error
potential \cite{claude-opus-47, claude-code}. We position this thesis
as an opportunistic extension of the U.S. Food and Drug Administration
28 April 2026 Real-Time Clinical Trials announcement
\cite{fda2026realtime} into the surgical theater, where a hypothetical
2030 4-arm Medtronic NeuroSpeed 1.0 closes 5x to 200x performance gaps
against the Medtronic ROSA ONE Brain v3.0 baseline
\cite{rosa-one-brain, Lefranc2014ROSAStereotaxy} on tip velocity
(1,000 vs 50 mm/s), acceleration (10,000 vs 200 mm/s squared), E-stop
latency (5 vs 50 ms), and force resolution (0.001 vs 0.01 N). The
54 column by 1001 row sensor sample table at
2030-gbm-1min/outputs/sensors/sensor_sample_4arm.csv, a 16-iteration
deterministic sweep, and an on-premises LLM tournament across
2030-gbm-1min/outputs/comparison/ and
2030-gbm-1min/outputs/comparison_robot_vs_human/ are checking steps
the industry no longer has to take \cite{repo-robotic-surgeries,
kawchak_2026_20113157}; practical real-life adoption still requires
bridging the synthetic patient gap and adopting TRIPOD+AI plus CREMLS
reporting floors.
