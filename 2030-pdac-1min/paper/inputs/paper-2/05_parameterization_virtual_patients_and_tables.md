  \section{Parameterization and Virtual Patient Population}  
  \label{sec:3}                 
\endgroup


\autoref{T1} presents a parameter catalog for selected high-impact model parameters, including their baseline values or priors, ranges (where applicable), and notes on data sources or sensitivity. All parameters were grounded in available clinical or preclinical data where possible, or chosen to reproduce known outcomes. For example, tumor growth rates ($r_{s}$, $r_{r}$) were set to yield a reasonable PDAC tumor doubling time (on the order of 1–2 months), consistent with clinical observations in metastatic PDAC. Chemotherapy kill rates were calibrated so that Arm A (chemo alone) produced response and survival outcomes matching historical trials. Novel agent parameters like the KRAS inhibitor’s maximum kill effect (Emax) and potency (EC50) were estimated from preclinical efficacy studies (assuming that at clinically achievable concentrations, the drug can substantially inhibit tumor growth). Immune-related parameters (baseline $k_{immune}$ kill rate, effect of PD-1 or CD40 therapies) were tuned to qualitatively match known immunotherapy behavior in PDAC (essentially no effect as monotherapy, modest effect in combination). The stromal barrier reduction by PEGPH20 was parameterized based on the HALO-301 trial: we set it such that, in $HA_{high}$ tumors, chemo penetration and efficacy improve slightly (reflected in a higher initial response rate) but not enough to significantly extend survival on its own.\\
\vspace{-0.1cm}

\textbf{Virtual Patient Variability:} A virtual patient cohort of 10,000 patients was generated by sampling parameter values to reflect inter-patient heterogeneity. Each virtual patient has a unique parameter set drawn from distributions. For example, the fraction of resistant cells $f_{res}$ varied between patients (most around 5\%, some higher or lower), initial immune strength varied (some patients have more robust baseline T-cell activity than others), and presence of biomarkers (KRAS G12D, KRAS G12C mutations, high stroma, high CD47 expression, germline BRCA mutation, etc.) was assigned according to real-world frequencies. Approximately 35–40\% of virtual patients were KRAS G12D-positive (matching PDAC genetics), \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde5\% were KRAS G12C-positive, \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde7\% had germline BRCA mutations, etc., ensuring the virtual population’s biomarker distribution mirrors reality. This means subpopulations corresponding to certain patient archetypes were present at realistic frequencies – for instance, \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde7\% ARCH-04 (BRCA-mutated, platinum-sensitive) patients, \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde5\% ARCH-05 (KRAS G12C-mutant) patients, and a sizeable fraction of ARCH-06 (stroma-high) tumors defined by the $HA_{high}$ marker. The benefit of a large cohort is that it captures rare subpopulations and yields robust statistics on outcomes (analogous to running a trial with thousands of patients per arm). Crucially, parameters were co-varied where appropriate: e.g. patients with high $f_{res}$ (intrinsically more resistant tumor) might also have a slightly higher tumor growth rate, representing more aggressive biology. This heterogeneity allowed identification of subgroups (responders vs. non-responders) and assessment of outcome variability. \\
\vspace{-0.1cm}

\textbf{Calibration Approach:} Model calibration was iterative and semi-automated. Key efficacy outputs (ORR, median OS) for control arms were matched to clinical data by adjusting parameters like chemo kill rate and tumor growth. For instance, gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel (Arm A) was tuned to \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde50\% ORR and \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde10 months OS in the model initially, a bit higher than the real MPACT trial (ORR \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde23\%, OS \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde8.5 mo), reflecting an “ideal” trial population. Gemcitabine monotherapy (simulated as a scenario for validation) gave \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde6.3 mo OS with ~0\% ORR, under the historical single-agent gemcitabine (\raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde6 month OS, \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde7\% ORR) within margin of error. The addition of magrolimab (Arm B) in the model roughly doubled the ORR (to \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde50\%) and added \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde1.5–2 months OS compared to chemo alone, which is biologically plausible but slightly optimistic (we assumed ideal full dosing and direct efficacy translation) – this was noted as a potential overestimate. Such calibration was done for each new mechanism: e.g. the PD-1 and CD40 effects were set so that PD-1 monotherapy would yield essentially 0\% ORR (matching clinical reality that checkpoint inhibitors alone have \raisebox{0.1ex}\textasciitilde0\% ORR in PDAC), while PD-1 + CD40 + chemo could yield a noticeable immune effect but at the cost of high toxicity (since clinically such combos have shown limited success). This iterative tuning (calibrate → simulate → compare to literature) ensured the parameter set produces face-valid results. All final parameter values and justifications were documented. \\
