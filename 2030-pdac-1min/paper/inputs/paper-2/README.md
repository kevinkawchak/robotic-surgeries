# README — Chunked Source Material for a New Physical AI Oncology Trial Paper

## Purpose of this README

This README is a navigation and cross-reference map for **ten chunked Markdown files** derived verbatim from a single source paper:

> **Kawchak K.** *QSP Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer AI Clinical Trial Simulation — From Protocol to Prediction: Code, VVUQ, and Playbook.* Zenodo. 2025. DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.17001137.

The chunks were produced by partitioning the original `main.tex` (1,250 lines) and `references.bib` (548 lines) into ten logically cohesive Markdown files. The LaTeX source text (including section commands, figure captions, table environments, equations, itemize lists, and inline math) has been preserved **word for word**; no section headings, sentences, numerical values, citations, or table cells have been abbreviated, reformatted, paraphrased, or added. Style-file (`PRIMEarxiv.sty`) preamble lines and image binaries were not included, per instructions.

This README is intended to be ingested by **Claude Opus 4.7 (1M context) Max** alongside the ten chunked `.md` files so that it can produce a **new physical AI oncology trial paper**. "Physical AI" in this downstream context refers to AI systems coupled to physical, mechanistic, or embodied simulation substrates — here exemplified by a Quantitative Systems Pharmacology (QSP) simulator whose ODE/PDE state evolves over a discretized 3-D tumor grid and a virtual patient population. The chunks supply (a) the **protocol-level clinical design**, (b) the **mechanistic model and equations**, (c) the **VVUQ evidence**, (d) the **comparative outcomes and clinical interpretation**, and (e) the **bibliographic substrate** that a successor paper can adapt, extend, cite, or critique.

### `01_title_and_abstract.md`
**Source: `main.tex` lines 83–148.**
Contains the paper's title — "QSP Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer AI Clinical Trial Simulation — From Protocol to Prediction: Code, VVUQ, and Playbook" — author block (Kevin Kawchak, ChemicalQDevice, San Diego, CA, dated August 29, 2025, with ORCID and contact e-mail), the `\maketitle` directive, and the full six-part structured Abstract organized as labeled paragraphs: **Question**, **Concepts**, **Results**, **Outputs**, **Impacts**, **Outcome**. Each paragraph is set inside a `\parbox` aligned to a custom `\labelwidth`. Headline content includes: the central question of whether AI can generate QSP pancreatic cancer trial protocols / Python scripts / VVUQ / playbook; the two prior author studies seeded into the protocol; the three primary AI models (ChatGPT 5 Pro Research, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Opus 4.1 Extended); the converged time step `dt = 0.05` and tumor volume grid size 5; comparisons to POLO, NAPOLI-1, and MPACT; the 10-arm / 7-archetype / 10,000-patient design with up to 250 ODEs per patient; the financial assessments contrasting industry QSP at \$2M and a typical Phase II at \$10.2M against the present study's \$36,304 theoretical cost (\$150/hr × 60 hr/wk × 4 weeks), yielding up to **99.6% cost reduction**, **23-month time reduction**, **+27,500% ROI**, and **\$3.6/patient vs \$59,500/patient (16,528×)**. The Abstract names Arm C (oncogene-targeted, ORR 70.8%, mOS 11.9 mo, HR = 0.50) as the most recommended combination and Arms H/I as consistently top performers, while flagging Arm E (mOS 6.6, Grade 3+ AEs 92.5%, Drop% 14.2%) as a no-go arm and noting ~2.5 million tumor-volume states updated per time-step. The Abstract ends with `\tableofcontents` and `\newpage`. This chunk is the orientation layer — every later chunk elaborates one phrase from here.

### `02_introduction_and_methods.md`
**Source: `main.tex` lines 152–196.**

### `06_implementation_verification_sensitivity.md`
**Source: `main.tex` lines 529–624.**
Section **4. Model Implementation and Reverse-Engineering** (Parts B and C of the project, the model stack on Python 3.12, Google Colab CPU and TPU instances with 13 GB and 173 GB RAM, ~50–100 adjustable parameters including ~20 core biophysical, ~10 toxicity/PK, with 2 dynamic state variables per compartment per patient — totaling ~2.5 million tumor-volume states at every time-step).
Section **5. Verification and Validation of QSP Clinical Trial** documents how Arm A ORR inflated from 0% at $dt = 0.05$ to ~5% at $dt = 0.25$, ~27% at $dt = 0.45$, and 31.5% at $dt = 0.50$ — pinpointing the numerical artifact and motivating the final $dt = 0.05$ choice. Spatial grid testing from GRID = 2 to 7 confirmed convergence at GRID = 5 with <1% variation up to GRID = 7. Figure 5A is referenced. The **Biological Validation** subsection cross-checks Arm G PD-1 monotherapy (0% ORR, rapid progression), Arm A chemo (~15% ORR, 6.9 mo OS).
Section **6. Sensitivity Analysis, Uncertainty Quantification** examines two parameters: **resistant fraction $f_{res}$** (Arm A ORR collapses from ~31.5% at 5% resistance to ~5.7% at 30% resistance — a six-fold decrease; Arms C and J show resilience because they bypass chemo-sensitivity dependence), and **KRAS-inhibitor potency $E_{max}$** (50% and 150% scaling moves Arm C ORR only from ~66.4% to ~71.9%, confirming saturation-regime calibration). Figures 6A and 6B are referenced. The chunk closes by noting supplementary folders B6 and B9 for further studies on resistant tumor volume derivatives and EC50 mrtx.

### `07_quality_efficiency_calibration.md`
**Source: `main.tex` lines 629–712.**

## How the chunks correlate

The ten chunks are organized so that a downstream reader can traverse them in approximately the same order as a reader of the original paper, but they cross-reference each other extensively. The principal correlations are:

**Vertical (top-down) dependence.** Chunk 1's Abstract states every headline finding that the rest of the chunks justify; chunk 2's Methods enumerates the AI tools whose hyperparameters appear in every later numerical claim; chunks 3 and 4 establish the clinical structure and equations whose parameters are populated in chunk 5; chunk 5's table FTS provides the raw outcome numbers (ORR, DCR, mPFS, mOS, G3+, Drop%) that chunks 8 and 9 interpret; chunk 5's table T1 supplies the parameter baselines that chunk 6 stresses; chunk 6's verification settles `dt = 0.05` and GRID = 5, which chunks 5 and 7 then assume; chunk 7's QA / ODE-counting numbers feed chunk 8's limitations and chunk 9's deliverables; chunk 9's data-availability tree corresponds one-to-one to the verification and sensitivity scans described in chunks 6 and 7 (`B4_Verify_Time_Step` ↔ chunk 6 Section 5; `B5_Verify_Grid_Size` ↔ chunk 6 Section 5; `B6`/`B7`/`B8`/`B9` ↔ chunk 6 Section 6).

**Numerical cross-links.** Arm-level numbers introduced in chunk 5's FTS table (Arm A ORR 14.7%, Arm C ORR 70.8%, Arm E mOS 6.6, Arm H drop 18.3%, Arm I drop 5.8%, etc.) recur in chunk 7's calibration section, chunk 8's findings/limitations, and chunk 1's Abstract — each citation in a different chunk should be interpreted as the same datum unless explicitly relabeled. The numerical-stability story (Arm A inflating from 14.7% at dt=0.05 to 31.5% at dt=0.50) threads through chunks 6 (Section 5), 7 (Section 9 noting refinement), and 8 (Section 11 limitations). The cost numbers (\$36,304; \$2M industry QSP; \$10.2M Phase II; 99.6% reduction; +27,500% ROI; 16,528× per-patient factor) recur in chunks 1, 7 (Section 8), and 9 (Section 14).

**Tabular and figure cross-links.** Figure 2A (workflow), 2B (modular architecture) live in chunk 4; Figure 5A (numerical convergence) in chunk 6; Figures 6A, 6B (sensitivity heatmap) in chunk 6; Figure 8A (runtime tradeoffs) in chunk 7; Figures 10A–10D (comparative efficacy waterfalls, KM, radar) in chunk 8; Figures 14A, 14B (financial assessments) in chunks 7 and 9. ## Guidance for a downstream "physical AI oncology trial paper"

A successor paper that extends or critiques this work can draw the following structural cues directly from the chunks:

- **Use chunks 1–2** to inherit the framing of LLM-augmented QSP workflows and the precedent of Project Optimus, MPACT, NAPOLI-1, POLO, FDA M15, and the Androulakis / Yang / Goryanin 2025 line of work.
- **Use chunks 3–5** as templates for arm/archetype definition, governing equations, parameter tables, and patient-log formats — including the CSV column schema (`patient_id, arm, line_of_therapy, archetype, age, sex, weight_kg, stage_iv, ecog, kras_g12d, kras_g12c, gbrca, ha_high, cd47_high, ca19_9, tumor_growth_rate, best_response, time_to_pfs_event, pfs_event_type, time_to_death_event, os_event_type, time_to_first_G3_AE, dose_adjusted, dropped_due_to_AE, auc_<drug> ×11`).
- **Use chunks 6–7** as the VVUQ scaffold: pair every claim with a convergence test (varying `dt`, varying GRID) and a sensitivity sweep (vary $f_{res}$, vary $E_{max}$ or analogous potency).
- **Use chunks 8–9** as the discussion/limitations/deliverables blueprint: identify which arms are no-go (high Drop%, low mOS, redundant mechanisms), which are biomarker-tractable (KRAS G12D, KRAS G12C, BRCA), and how to translate model outcomes into prospective recommendations and economics.
- **Use chunk 10** as the citation pool. The Kawchak-series back-references, the QSP and FDA-MIDD references, and the three clinical anchor papers (`01PaperFolfironox`, `02PaperGem`, `03PapernalIRI5FU`) should be carried forward as foundational.

When extending to a "physical AI" framing, the bridge from this paper is the **closure of the loop between mechanistic simulator state (the ~$10^6$-ODE tumor system) and AI control / generation (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude Opus performing code drafting, hyperparameter tuning, and reverse-engineering)**. The successor paper can deepen this by adding (a) embodied or robotic data-collection layers, (b) closed-loop sensor → simulator → AI → protocol updates, (c) real-time digital-twin synchronization, or (d) hardware-accelerated agentic VVUQ — each natural extensions of the present design that are explicitly anticipated in chunk 8's Section 12 future-work list.
