# Introduction

## Clinical Trials & Digital Twins

Digital twins are composed of artificial intelligence layers that build, run, and learn simulated clinical trials. AI has been used in the design and cohort selection process which utilized multi-modal data fusion and mechanistic hybrid modeling [06IntroBordukova]. Further in silico protocol design and intelligent patient stratification were employed prior to executing and adapting a trial. Several what-if scenarios can be proposed in real-time while predictive monitoring and safety of adverse events are conducted [07IntroWang], with adaptive trial designs being an option. Additionally, post-trial analysis can be processed by AI for causal analysis and novel biomarker discovery. In October 2024, funding for the technology increased with "NSF, NIH and FDA support[ing] research in digital twin technology for biomedical applications." $6M across seven projects focused on areas including virtual patient cohorts and first-principles informed data-enabled prediction for human physiology [12IntroNSFNIHFDA, 13IntroNSFNIHFDA].

## Digital Twins in Oncology

In April 2022, *Applied Clinical Trials* featured a team of three members from Phesi that developed a digital twin to address untreatable KRAS mutations of NSCLC Patients with standard of care. The analysis measured median Progression-Free Survival (mPFS) using data from 462,538 NSCLC patients to potentially help aid in the design of clinical trials. 1,298 NSCLC patients with the KRAS mutation were identified from predominantly Phase II trials to identify "a population of NSCLC patients with KRAS G12C mutation would yield a mPFS of about 3.0 months, when treated with Standard of Care (SOC) [02IntroPhesi, 03IntroPhesi]."

In March 2025, Atlis Labs' "AI Model Evaluation on Data from Phase 3 Lung Cancer Trial at ELCC 2025" illustrated how ingested data was used to create virtual representations of patient tumor images used to predict future outcomes such as overall survival and progression-free survival more accurately than the current clinical standard RECIST 1.1. "Responders per IPRO-Δ had significantly better OS compared to subjects with IPRO-Δ stable disease (HR=0.54, 95% CI: 0.31-0.92), while responders per RECIST 1.1 had no better OS than subjects with RECIST stable disease (HR=1.10, 95% CI: 0.70, 1.77)." "The findings support the growing evidence that AI-powered imaging endpoints have the potential to enhance drug developers' ability to objectively predict differences in long-term outcomes of clinical trial subjects." Although not a full digital twin, the development represents an organ system simulation useful as a computational, predictive model that serves as a foundational building block [04IntroAltislabs].

In April 2025, Wu et al. utilized MRI-based digital twins to improve treatment response of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) by optimizing neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens. The authors performed this task by employing 105 TNBC patients from the ARTEMIS trial (NCT02276443) who received Adriamycin/Cytoxan (A/C)-Taxol (T). "Digital twins were established by calibrating a biology-based mathematical model to patient-specific MRI data, which accurately predicted pathological complete response (pCR) with an AUC of 0.82." The authors then "used each patient's twin to theoretically optimize outcome by identifying their optimal A/C-T schedule from 128 options. The patient-specific optimized treatment yielded a significant improvement in pCR rate of 20.95–24.76% [05IntroWu]."

## PDAC Digital Twin Initiatives

Frederick National Laboratory featured a project titled "Simulating One Million Pancreatic Cancer Patient Digital Twins to Plan Precision Medicine Treatment Strategies and Improve Long-term Survival." The project aim by McCoy et al. in 2020 focused on parameterizing inputs from real patient trajectories regarding drug sensitivity and resistance, and was joined by four other cancer patient digital twin team initiatives [08IntroFrederick, 09IntroFrederick].

A January 2024 *Nature Cancer* researchers as Cedars-Sinai Medical Center and Johns Hopkins University published a paper titled "The Molecular Twin artificial-intelligence platform integrates multi-omic data to predict outcomes for pancreatic adenocarcinoma patients", which utilized a "dataset of 6,363 clinical and multi-omic molecular features from patients with resected pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma to accurately predict disease survival (DS)". The authors showed that "a full multi-omic model predicts DS with the highest accuracy and that plasma protein is the top single-omic predictor of DS." In addition, the authors' "platform enables discovery of parsimonious biomarker panels and performance assessment of outcome prediction models learning from resource-intensive panels [10IntroOsipov]."

In January 2024, researchers at Genentech published: "Digital twins elucidate critical role of Tscm in clinical persistence of TCR-engineered cell therapy". The authors "developed a novel quantitative systems pharmacology (QSP) model of TCR-transgenic T cell therapy in patients with solid tumors to describe the kinetics of endogenous T cells and multiple memory subsets of engineered T cells after infusion." The authors "simulated in silico clinical trials using digital twins and predict that Tscm enrichment in the infused product improves persistence of the engineered T cells and could enable administration of a lower dose.", and "verified the broader relevance of the QSP model, the digital twins, and findings on the importance of Tscm enrichment by predicting kinetics for two patients with pancreatic cancer treated with KRAS G12D targeting T cell therapy [11IntroJoslyn]."

While previous digital twin developments have demonstrated effective methods to significantly increase population size [08IntroFrederick], incorporate multi-omic data [10IntroOsipov], and utilize kinetics in models [11IntroJoslyn], they were found to lack state-of-the art AI contextually aware models, and subsequently lacked generalizability across many clinical trial papers. To address these limitations, a novel digital twin AI framework was developed that provided end-to-end integration of clinical trial literature using LLMs to generate proposals for advanced predictive modeling and clinical translation success. This study reveals the steps of data mining using meta-analyses, summarizations with reports, analysis, charting, and visual comparisons throughout the study. This multi-step workflow was performed in order to obtain three competitive AI-informed PDAC digital twin proposals.

# Methods

## Part I:

o3re meta-analyses were obtained using Standard A Prompts 01-40 composed of nine sections (Abstract, Plain-language summary, Background, Objectives, Methods, Results, Authors' conclusions, "Summary of Findings", and Appendices) shown in Standard A (StandardAa). Full prompts, generations, research summaries, and citations are available in Supplementary 01-40. Each meta-analysis was combined into a 408,081 word Dataset 1, with beginning and ending markers for each of the forty outputs. The generations were divided into four files due to character limitations, as represented by Supplementary Dataset 1A-1D.

One verification for each of the meta-analyses for a total of 40 verifications compared to Dataset 1 was performed using g25p web-based searches of trial literature and utilizing similarity and scores to yield comparisons as depicted in Standard B Prompt 41 in StandardB. Text from all datasets were copied from plain text pdfs derived from Google Docs [GoogleDocs]. Prompts included in the paper were formatted using LaTeX, therefore slight formatting differences may exist from supplementary files. 67 prompts used in the study are cross-referenced to respective supplementary files in the Appendix.

## Part II:

Six reports were generated by g25p based on the Standard C prompt template with prompts 42-47, and Dataset 1. The reports were then combined to form Dataset 2. At least one table from each of the reports was verified against Dataset 1 using g25p to obtain an auditable table seen in Standard D Prompts 48-53 shown in StandardDa and StandardDb. Standard E Prompts 54-62 with individual report tables were converted into visualization scripts by son4, and were subsequently processed in Google Colab [GoogleColab], and analyzed with Visual Studio Code [Visual_Studio_Code]. If an error was present in the script, the cell contents followed by the Python error code were pasted into grk3, and corrected code was included with grk3 explanations in markdown in Python notebooks.

## Parts III-IV:

Proposals were generated individually by o3pr, ops4, g25p, o3ch, and grk3 using the same Prompt 63 and Dataset 2 for each model to yield five PDAC digital twin proposals shown in Standard F StandardF. All five proposals were evaluated together by the five models separately according to Prompt 64 in Standard F. A scoring system was used to determine each proposal's funding probability, and the top three proposals were used in further visualizations. Charts from these proposals were generated by ops4 and son4 to yield head-to-head financial comparisons.
