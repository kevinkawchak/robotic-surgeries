# Results: Meta-Analyses

Output lengths for meta-analyses were recorded in MAsWordsMinutes, with an average of 10,196 words, minimum of 6,440 words, maximum of 13,033 words, and a total of 407,841 words (408,081 words as Dataset 1). Run times averaged 19.32 minutes, with a standard deviation of 4.18, and totaled 772.89 minutes or 12.88 hours. Overall words per minute reach 527.6, with a Pearson's r coefficient for words vs. minutes of -0.127, indicating a low correlation between word count and run time. With meta-analyses 01, 11, 14, and 22 the status bar was complete at ~28 minutes; and after restarting the software, the output was normal.

Each of the 40 meta-analyses was based on a different PDAC research area prompt which were implemented into the Standard A template and run separately, as shown in StandardAa and StandardAb. The template was optimized further for this disease type from a prior study [17KawchakGlioblastoma], with modifications aimed at obtaining larger tables, a data-lineage map, traffic-light risk-of-bias grid, timeline lane (swim-lane) table, and an automated audit log. The prompt template updates permitted for each of the complete PRISMA-2020- aligned audit-ready meta-analyses to be accessible for external analysis. In addition, the prompt template enabled deeper heterogeneity statistics, stricter traceability, and clearer prioritization rules over a prior study [17KawchakGlioblastoma].

Individual meta-analysis supplementary files have notes that include the number of sections that were included in the output. 39/40 (97.5%) of the meta-analyses contained all 9 sections required, while a limited number of analyses changed the order or slightly modified section headings. Performant behavior was observed according the prompt template across meta-analyses. For instance MA01 contained larger tables of PRISMA flow, audit log, and pooled effects. MA13's Appendix 5 included individual trial swim-lane timelines for two clinical trials, while the MA26 Authors' Conclusions section by AI included a correct statement regarding the challenging PDAC disease "The collective evidence from 2010–2025 indicates that CDK4/6 inhibitors have not achieved any clinical efficacy breakthrough in advanced PDAC."

# Results: Reports

Reports were independently generated by g25p from Dataset 1, with a range of 1,807 to 3,304 words and 2.24 to 2.67 minutes in ReportsWordsMinutes. Only g25p of the models tested had the large 1M token context length to process the 408,081 words at 753,489 tokens. The resulting six reports had a run time of 14.86 minutes for a total of 1,024.6 words per minute. Combined reports totaled 15,348 words, which represents Dataset 2. Unlike the meta-analysis generations, the Pearson's r coefficient between words and minutes was high, at 0.94. This indicates that for similar tasks, as run time was longer, so was the report length.

Standard C includes the prompt template for the six reports required for processing of the 40 meta-analyses in Dataset 1 to include relevant tables that "must be structured to serve as direct inputs for visualizations", and "must especially include long-range relationships across different meta-analyses." In addition, sample calculations of new metrics while not including external clinical trials was a condition. Prompts 42-47 applied separately into the template were obtained through meta-prompts that identified the key areas useful for the report objectives. These 6 areas primarily focused on therapeutic scores, exceptional & orphan responders, longitudinal treatments, a failed therapeutic, an adaptive trial, and digital twin trial parameterization.

"Daraxonrasib + Mitazalimab + Liposomal Irinotecan", the top-ranked therapeutic triplicate used in all 5 proposals was derived from Prompt 42/Report 01 Table 2 as having the highest TSVS score of 8.15. This Pan-KRAS Inhibitor + CD40 Agonist combination was further rationalized with efficacy/toxicity/MoA/biomarker component contributions. In addition, the Prompt 47/Report 06 Table 1 featuring 7 "Virtual Patient Profiles" were directly cited in Proposal A population archetypes to specifically implement the "Seven archetypes from Report 6 (ARCH‑01 to ARCH‑07) covering age, fitness, stage, KRAS alleles, BRCA/DDR status and stromal phenotypes." Report 6 is included, which also depicts Treatment Impact Parameters that serve as the core input for the simulation's treatment module, which are mandatory for use in Proposal A's Digital-Twin Architecture "82 ODEs; humanised from Report 6 INT‑series parameters".

Report verifications shown in ReportsWordsMinutes were based on utilizing a single table from a report against Dataset 1 as shown in StandardDa and StandardDb. The task could only be completed at this scale by g25p due to its large context window and higher recall accuracy [01PaperBehrouz] compared to other models tested. Average values for words across the verifications was 2,397 at 2.33 minutes, with a standard deviation of 0.22. A total of 1,028.8 words per minute were reached, with a Pearson's r coefficient of 0.75. Verification prompts in StandardDa illustrate the method of verifying a report table compared against Dataset 1, followed by a new verification table generating.

Instructions to "review your analysis and identify any errors or oversights that were made" were provided in efforts to improve the quality of the final verification table. As an example, Standard D Prompt 53 in StandardDb for Report 06 Table 1 specified a table format identified using meta-prompts, followed by Similarity classifications and a score of 1=Exact, 0.8=Close, 0.7=Partial, 0.5=Error. AI was then instructed to calculate average and standard deviation scores for the auditable verification table. The analysis made by g25p in Report06_Verify for archetype data points illustrate values found in reports were compared to the specific meta-analysis and respective values, followed by similarities and scores. The source data goes back to the meta-analyses, and not to the original articles due to some limitations in grounding multiple pieces of information at scale.
