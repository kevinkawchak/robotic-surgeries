# 1. Executive baseline

As of May 12, 2026, daraxonrasib is Revolution Medicines' oral, noncovalent RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor being advanced most prominently for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, with a positive randomized Phase 3 readout in previously treated metastatic PDAC and additional first-line and adjuvant Phase 3 trials underway. [1]

The most important 2026 event is the April 13, 2026 topline announcement that RASolute 302 met all primary and key secondary endpoints, including progression-free survival and overall survival, in previously treated metastatic PDAC. [1]

In the RASolute 302 intent-to-treat population, median overall survival was 13.2 months with once-daily oral daraxonrasib versus 6.7 months with standard intravenous cytotoxic chemotherapy, corresponding to a hazard ratio of 0.40 and p value below 0.0001. [1]

RASolute 302 randomized patients to 300 mg daraxonrasib once daily versus investigator's choice standard-of-care chemotherapy, enrolled tumors with RAS G12 mutations such as G12D, G12V, and G12R as well as tumors without an identified RAS mutation, and used blinded independent central review progression-free survival and overall survival in the RAS G12 population as primary endpoints. [2]

FDA issued a safe-to-proceed letter on May 1, 2026, allowing Revolution Medicines to initiate an expanded access treatment protocol for daraxonrasib in previously treated metastatic PDAC. [3]

The peer-reviewed Phase 1-2 PDAC cohort publication in the New England Journal of Medicine reported that daraxonrasib produced objective responses and durable disease control signals in previously treated RAS-mutated PDAC, while treatment-related grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 30% of 168 patients treated at doses up to 300 mg. [4]

The active pancreatic cancer Phase 3 program comprises RASolute 302 in previously treated metastatic PDAC, RASolute 303 in first-line metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, and RASolute 304 in resected PDAC after perioperative chemotherapy. [5]

The 2026 baseline interpretation is that daraxonrasib has moved from promising early RAS biology and single-arm clinical activity to the first reported broad RAS-targeted randomized Phase 3 survival-positive program in metastatic PDAC, while regulatory approval and complete peer-reviewed Phase 3 data remain pending. [1]

# 2. Disease rationale and mechanism

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is a RAS-driven disease context in which activating RAS mutations occur in more than 90% of tumors, making direct RAS pathway intervention a central therapeutic objective. [4]

Daraxonrasib was designed to inhibit the active, guanosine triphosphate-bound RAS(ON) state across mutant and wild-type canonical RAS isoforms rather than targeting a single covalent KRAS allele. [6]

The drug forms a tri-complex with cyclophilin A and RAS and blocks RAS interaction with downstream effectors, a mechanism intended to suppress signaling from multiple RAS variants relevant to PDAC. [7]

The translational rationale was supported by preclinical work showing potent anticancer activity across RAS-addicted cell lines, particularly KRAS codon 12 models, and profound regressions in KRAS G12X xenograft models after oral dosing. [6]

The translational development plan predicted 300 mg once daily as an exposure level associated with antitumor responses, which later aligned with the Phase 3 daraxonrasib dose used in RASolute 302. [6]

Because daraxonrasib is multi-selective rather than G12D-only or G12C-only, its clinical hypothesis is broader than single-allele KRAS inhibition and includes the possibility of activity across common pancreatic cancer subtypes such as G12D, G12V, and G12R. [2]
