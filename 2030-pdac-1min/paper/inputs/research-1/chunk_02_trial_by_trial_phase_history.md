# 4. Trial-by-trial clinical phase history

## 4.1 RMC-6236-001: Phase 1/2 monotherapy foundation

RMC-6236-001 is a multicenter, open-label Phase 1/2 trial that evaluates safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, dose-limiting toxicity, and clinical activity of oral RMC-6236 in advanced solid tumors with RAS alterations. [8]

The ClinicalTrials.gov record lists the study as recruiting, with actual start on May 31, 2022, estimated primary completion on May 31, 2027, estimated study completion on July 26, 2027, and estimated enrollment of 754 participants. [8]

Eligibility includes advanced solid tumors with specific KRAS G12 mutations in dose escalation, broader RAS mutations in dose optimization and expansion, and a PDAC wild-type RAS expansion cohort. [8]

Early ESMO 2023 data described 111 patients with NSCLC or PDAC treated once daily from 80 mg to 400 mg, including 65 PDAC patients with a median of three prior lines of therapy. [9]

Among 46 efficacy-evaluable PDAC patients in the ESMO 2023 dataset, Revolution Medicines reported a 20% objective response rate and 87% disease control rate, with confirmed responses observed in KRAS G12D, G12V, and G12R tumors. [9]

The same early dataset described rash and gastrointestinal adverse events as the most common treatment-related events, mostly grade 1 or 2, with grade 3 treatment-related rash in 6%, stomatitis in 2%, and diarrhea in 1% of patients. [9]

The NEJM Phase 1-2 publication reported that 300 mg once daily was selected as the Phase 3 dose after patients received daily doses from 10 mg to 400 mg in the early-phase program. [4]

In the NEJM PDAC analysis, 168 previously treated RAS-mutated PDAC patients treated at doses up to 300 mg experienced any-grade treatment-related adverse events in 96% of cases and grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events in 30% of cases. [4]

The most common treatment-related adverse events reported in the NEJM abstract included rash, diarrhea, nausea, stomatitis or mucositis, vomiting, and fatigue. [4]

In 26 second-line RAS G12 PDAC patients treated with 300 mg, the NEJM report showed an objective response rate of 35%, median duration of response of 8.2 months, median progression-free survival of 8.5 months, and median overall survival of 13.1 months. [4]

In 38 second-line patients with RAS G12, G13, or Q61 mutations treated with 300 mg, the NEJM report showed an objective response rate of 29%, median duration of response of 8.2 months, median progression-free survival of 8.1 months, and median overall survival of 15.6 months. [4]

The NEJM analysis is important to this baseline because it is the first major peer-reviewed clinical publication connecting the daraxonrasib mechanism to dose, tolerability, response, and survival signals in PDAC. [4]

## 4.2 RMC-GI-102: Phase 1/2 combination platform in gastrointestinal tumors

RMC-GI-102 is a Phase 1/2 open-label multicenter study of RAS(ON) inhibitor combinations in gastrointestinal solid tumors, including PDAC cohorts. [19]

The ClinicalTrials.gov record for NCT06445062 was last updated on April 1, 2026, and lists Revolution Medicines as sponsor. [19]

At AACR 2026, Revolution Medicines reported first-line metastatic PDAC data from a daraxonrasib plus gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel cohort and a daraxonrasib monotherapy cohort. [18]

In the combination cohort, 40 previously untreated RAS-mutant metastatic PDAC patients received daraxonrasib 200 mg once daily in 28-day cycles with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel on Days 1 and 15, with a December 1, 2025 data cutoff. [18]

In that combination cohort, the most common grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events were anemia in 33%, decreased neutrophil count in 20%, and fatigue in 18%, and no grade 5 treatment-related adverse events were reported. [18]

The combination cohort produced a confirmed objective response rate of 58% with one complete response, while median progression-free survival and median overall survival were not mature at the data cutoff. [18]

The same AACR update estimated six-month progression-free survival at 84% and six-month overall survival at 90% for the combination cohort. [18]

The first-line monotherapy cohort from RMC-6236-001 used 300 mg once daily in 21-day cycles and reported all-grade treatment-related adverse events in 95% and grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events in 38%, with no grade 4 or grade 5 treatment-related adverse events. [18]

The first-line monotherapy cohort reported an objective response rate of 47%, a disease control rate of 92%, one complete response, six-month progression-free survival of 71%, and six-month overall survival of 83%, while median progression-free survival and median overall survival were not mature. [18]

These first-line datasets do not substitute for randomized Phase 3 evidence, but they provide the clinical bridge supporting RASolute 303 in untreated metastatic PDAC. [18]

## 4.3 RASolute 302: Phase 3 previously treated metastatic PDAC

RASolute 302 is the pivotal, global, open-label, randomized Phase 3 trial of daraxonrasib versus investigator's choice standard-of-care chemotherapy in previously treated metastatic PDAC. [10]

ClinicalTrials.gov lists RASolute 302 as active, not recruiting, with actual study start on October 16, 2024, actual enrollment of 500 participants, estimated primary completion in June 2026, and estimated study completion in December 2027. [10]

The trial randomizes patients 1:1 to oral RMC-6236 or investigator's choice chemotherapy after one prior line of 5-fluorouracil-based or gemcitabine-based therapy. [10]

Revolution Medicines stated that the Phase 3 trial used 300 mg daraxonrasib once daily versus investigator's choice standard cytotoxic chemotherapy. [2]

The enrolled population included metastatic PDAC harboring a wide range of RAS variants, including RAS G12D, G12V, and G12R, as well as patients without an identified tumor RAS mutation. [2]

Primary endpoints were progression-free survival by blinded independent central review and overall survival in patients with RAS G12 tumors, while secondary endpoints included PFS and OS in the intent-to-treat population, objective response rate, duration of response, and patient-reported quality of life. [2]

On April 13, 2026, Revolution Medicines announced that RASolute 302 met all primary and key secondary endpoints, including statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvements in progression-free survival and overall survival. [1]

The topline intent-to-treat overall survival result was 13.2 months with daraxonrasib versus 6.7 months with standard chemotherapy, with a hazard ratio of 0.40 and p value below 0.0001. [1]

The company reported that daraxonrasib was generally well tolerated in RASolute 302, had a manageable safety profile, and showed no new safety signals in the topline analysis. [1]

Revolution Medicines stated that all PFS and OS endpoint results from the first interim analysis are considered final, which makes the April 2026 update a definitive efficacy milestone rather than a preliminary trend. [1]

Detailed RASolute 302 results, including additional efficacy and safety analyses, are scheduled for presentation in the ASCO 2026 Plenary Session on May 31, 2026, after the May 12, 2026 baseline date of this document. [2]

## 4.4 RASolute 303: Phase 3 first-line metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma

RASolute 303 is a global, open-label, randomized, three-arm Phase 3 trial evaluating daraxonrasib monotherapy or daraxonrasib plus gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel against gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel as first-line treatment for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma. [20]

ClinicalTrials.gov lists RASolute 303 as recruiting, with actual study start on March 9, 2026, estimated enrollment of 900 participants, estimated primary completion in June 2028, and estimated study completion in March 2029. [20]

Revolution Medicines announced on April 2, 2026, that it had begun treating patients in RASolute 303, and the company stated that the study enrolls patients irrespective of tumor RAS genotype. [17]

RASolute 303 is designed to test whether daraxonrasib alone or in combination with gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel can improve progression-free survival or overall survival compared with standard gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel in previously untreated metastatic disease. [20]

The first-line Phase 1/2 monotherapy and combination results reported at AACR 2026 support the biological and clinical rationale for RASolute 303, but the randomized Phase 3 trial remains the decisive evidence generator for this setting. [18]

## 4.5 RASolute 304: Phase 3 adjuvant/resected PDAC

RASolute 304 is a global, randomized, open-label, two-arm Phase 3 study of adjuvant daraxonrasib versus standard-of-care observation after completion of perioperative chemotherapy in patients with resected PDAC. [21]

ClinicalTrials.gov lists RASolute 304 as recruiting, with actual study start on December 15, 2025, estimated enrollment of 500 participants, estimated primary completion on May 10, 2029, and estimated study completion on July 10, 2030. [21]

Eligible patients must have histologically confirmed PDAC with R0 or R1 curative-intent surgical resection, no recurrent or metastatic disease, completed perioperative multi-agent chemotherapy within the prior 12 weeks, adequate organ function, ECOG 0 or 1, and documented RAS mutation status. [21]

The primary endpoint is investigator-assessed disease-free survival, and secondary endpoints include overall survival, blinded independent central review disease-free survival, one-year and two-year DFS and OS rates, safety and tolerability, and pharmacokinetics. [21]

Revolution Medicines announced the first patient randomized in RASolute 304 on December 18, 2025, making adjuvant development part of the daraxonrasib pancreatic cancer program before the pivotal metastatic Phase 3 readout. [15]

The adjuvant rationale is strategically important because it tests whether direct RAS(ON) inhibition can suppress recurrence risk after surgery and chemotherapy, not just treat established metastatic disease. [21]
