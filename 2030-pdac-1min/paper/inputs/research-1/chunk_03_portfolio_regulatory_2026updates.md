# 6. Regulatory status and access pathway

Daraxonrasib has FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation for previously treated metastatic PDAC harboring KRAS G12 mutations, based on promising early clinical evidence in a serious disease with unmet need. [12]

Daraxonrasib has FDA Orphan Drug Designation for pancreatic cancer, a status that can provide development incentives and potential market exclusivity if the drug is approved for the orphan indication. [14]

FDA selected daraxonrasib for the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot program in October 2025, and FDA describes the pilot as a non-transferable pathway intended to shorten review timelines for selected applications while maintaining the statutory safety and effectiveness standard. [13]

FDA's CNPV program materials describe an intended one-to-two-month review timeline for selected applications compared with typical standard or priority review timelines, with enhanced communication and rolling-review features. [22]

On May 1, 2026, FDA announced that it had issued a safe-to-proceed letter allowing Revolution Medicines to initiate an expanded access treatment protocol for daraxonrasib in previously treated metastatic PDAC. [3]

FDA received the expanded access request from Revolution Medicines on April 28, 2026, and signed it on April 30, 2026. [3]

FDA stated that expanded access requests under the daraxonrasib treatment protocol must be submitted to the sponsor by U.S.-licensed physicians on behalf of eligible patients. [3]

Revolution Medicines has stated that it does not accept direct expanded-access requests from patients or caregivers and that licensed treating physicians should initiate requests through the company's medical information process. [23]

Revolution Medicines reported on May 6, 2026, that it intends to submit daraxonrasib data to global regulatory authorities and to use the CNPV path for the U.S. NDA, but no public approval had occurred by the May 12, 2026 baseline date. [24]

Revolution Medicines also reported a positive European Medicines Agency opinion on Orphan Drug Designation for daraxonrasib in pancreatic cancer in its first-quarter 2026 update. [24]

# 7. 2026 updates and breakthroughs current through May 12, 2026

The February 25, 2026 corporate update positioned daraxonrasib as one of the company's multiple ongoing Phase 3 assets and stated that RASolute 302 had completed global enrollment with a readout expected in the first half of 2026. [16]

The same February 2026 update stated that RASolute 303 initiation was underway in first-line metastatic PDAC and RASolute 304 enrollment was ongoing in the adjuvant setting. [16]

The April 2, 2026 RASolute 303 update confirmed that first-line metastatic PDAC treatment had begun in the global randomized Phase 3 trial. [17]

The April 13, 2026 RASolute 302 update is the major 2026 breakthrough because it changed daraxonrasib from a candidate supported by single-arm early-phase activity into a candidate with randomized Phase 3 evidence of improved overall survival versus chemotherapy. [1]

The April 21, 2026 ASCO announcement established that the first public detailed Phase 3 presentation would occur after this baseline date, so any post-May 12 interpretations should be updated after the May 31, 2026 ASCO Plenary Session. [2]

The April 21, 2026 AACR update showed that first-line daraxonrasib monotherapy and daraxonrasib plus gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel cohorts had meaningful preliminary activity, but the data remained nonrandomized and immature for median PFS and OS. [18]

The May 1, 2026 FDA expanded access action created a practical access bridge for eligible U.S. patients with previously treated metastatic PDAC while formal review remains pending. [3]

The May 6-7, 2026 publication cycle added peer-reviewed NEJM support for daraxonrasib's Phase 1-2 safety and activity profile, increasing the quality of the evidence base available before ASCO's Phase 3 presentation. [4]

As of May 12, 2026, the baseline should treat the RASolute 302 Phase 3 efficacy result as final for PFS and OS according to the sponsor, but it should still treat detailed subgroup results, quality-of-life outcomes, and granular safety tables as pending until the ASCO presentation or a full Phase 3 manuscript.
