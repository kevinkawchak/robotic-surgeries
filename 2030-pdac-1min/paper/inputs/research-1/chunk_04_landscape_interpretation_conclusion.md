# 8. External and adjacent RAS-targeted pancreatic cancer landscape

The pancreatic cancer RAS landscape outside daraxonrasib is moving quickly, but as of May 12, 2026 daraxonrasib is differentiated by having a positive randomized Phase 3 survival result in metastatic PDAC rather than only early-phase evidence. [1]

Sotorasib provided earlier clinical proof that direct KRAS inhibition could produce responses in KRAS G12C-mutated pancreatic cancer, but KRAS G12C is a small subset of PDAC and the NEJM CodeBreaK 100 pancreatic cancer cohort reported an objective response rate of 21%. [25]

MRTX1133, a KRAS G12D inhibitor originally developed by Mirati and then Bristol Myers Squibb, entered a Phase 1/2 trial in advanced solid tumors with KRAS G12D but was terminated because of formulation challenges before Phase 2 began. [26]

Astellas' setidegrasib, also known as ASP3082, is a KRAS G12D-targeted protein degrader that moved into a Phase 3 registrational study in April 2026 as first-line treatment in KRAS G12D-mutated metastatic PDAC in combination with mFOLFIRINOX or NALIRIFOX. [27]

Astellas' Phase 3 setidegrasib study is randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, plans to enroll more than 600 participants, and uses overall survival as the primary endpoint. [27]

Eli Lilly's LY3962673 is being evaluated in MOONRAY-01, a Phase 1a/1b trial in KRAS G12D-mutant solid tumors including PDAC, with monotherapy and combination cohorts and estimated enrollment of 630 participants. [28]

Hengrui's HRS-4642 is a Phase 1 KRAS G12D program in advanced solid tumors, with ClinicalTrials.gov listing active-not-recruiting status, actual enrollment of 102, and an actual primary completion date of June 25, 2024. [29]

Revolution Medicines is also advancing zoldonrasib, or RMC-9805, an oral RAS(ON) G12D inhibitor, and the company reported in February 2026 that it had initiated RASolute 305 in first-line metastatic PDAC harboring RAS G12D. [16]

The external landscape matters analytically because daraxonrasib's multi-selective RAS(ON) approach may address a broader PDAC population, while G12D-selective inhibitors and degraders may compete strongly in the largest individual KRAS subtype. [2]

The key unresolved comparative question is not simply whether each agent can shrink tumors, but whether each strategy can generate durable overall survival, manageable chronic toxicity, resistance control, and feasible combinations with modern chemotherapy backbones. [27]

# 9. Baseline interpretation for downstream AI analysis

Evidence strength is now highest for second-line or previously treated metastatic PDAC because that is the setting with a completed positive randomized Phase 3 readout and FDA expanded access protocol. [1]

Evidence strength is moderate but still immature in first-line metastatic PDAC because Phase 1/2 monotherapy and combination data are encouraging, RASolute 303 is active, and randomized results are not expected until later. [18]

Evidence strength is early in resected/adjuvant PDAC because RASolute 304 has begun and is recruiting, but recurrence-prevention efficacy data are years away. [21]

The most important efficacy anchor is the RASolute 302 overall survival hazard ratio of 0.40 in the intent-to-treat population, but the analysis should be updated after ASCO 2026 to capture RAS G12 primary population outcomes, progression-free survival magnitude, objective response, duration of response, adverse event tables, and quality-of-life findings. [1]

The most important safety anchor is the early-phase NEJM report showing frequent treatment-related adverse events, especially rash and gastrointestinal or mucosal events, with grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events in approximately one third of previously treated PDAC patients. [4]

The main regulatory anchor is that daraxonrasib has Breakthrough Therapy designation, Orphan Drug Designation, CNPV selection, and FDA-authorized expanded access, but no FDA approval or label as of May 12, 2026. [3]

The most important market and competitive anchor is that daraxonrasib's broad RAS(ON) mechanism competes not only with chemotherapy but also with emerging allele-specific KRAS G12D inhibitors and degraders that may be developed in overlapping first-line metastatic PDAC populations. [27]

The main biological risk for all direct RAS strategies remains acquired resistance, incomplete pathway suppression, or compensatory signaling, which makes combination therapy and sequencing analyses central future questions. [6]

The main clinical operations risk is whether patient eligibility, mutational testing, expanded access logistics, chronic adverse event management, and eventual payer or regulatory constraints will affect real-world uptake after any approval. [3]

# 11. Bottom-line conclusion

Daraxonrasib entered May 2026 as the most clinically advanced broad RAS(ON) multi-selective pancreatic cancer candidate, with the RASolute 302 Phase 3 readout establishing a survival-positive randomized benchmark in previously treated metastatic PDAC. [1]

The program now spans a Phase 3 continuum from previously treated metastatic disease to first-line metastatic disease and resected/adjuvant disease, which makes daraxonrasib a platform-level pancreatic cancer asset rather than a single-trial candidate. [5]

The right baseline stance is therefore optimistic but not complete: the efficacy signal is unusually strong for metastatic PDAC, regulatory and expanded-access momentum is substantial, and the next decisive updates are detailed Phase 3 disclosure, NDA activity, approval status, and randomized results from earlier-line studies.
