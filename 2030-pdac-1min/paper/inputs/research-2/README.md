## Detailed file descriptions

### chunk_01_header_executive_summary_sections_1_to_4.md

**What it contains:**  
The full document title, subtitle, dateline, authorship note, and disclaimer. The complete executive summary (17 paragraphs), which functions as a high-density synopsis of the entire evidence base. Followed by four numbered sections:

- **Section 1 — Scope and definitions:** Defines the Whipple procedure operationally, distinguishes classic from pylorus-preserving pancreaticoduodenectomy, introduces the four-domain success framework (perioperative, oncologic, functional, health-system), and specifies that the minimum AI-baseline outcome window is 90 days.
- **Section 2 — Surgical history and evolution:** Traces the procedure from Whipple, Parsons, and Mullins (1935) through staged-to-one-stage transition, centralization, Medicare-era volume-outcome data, and 21st-century technical diversification.
- **Section 3 — Patient selection and resectability in 2026:** Covers the anatomic-biologic-treatment resectability framework, the role of multiagent neoadjuvant chemotherapy (PREOPANC, ESPAC-5F, A021501, NORPACT-1), the 2026 Mayo/JNCCN vein-abutment reclassification, and inputs required for future AI-assisted patient-selection tools.
- **Section 4 — Current operative goals and technical milestones:** Details the four oncologic goals of the operation, key technical steps, the primacy of the pancreatic anastomosis, Fistula Risk Score and alternative Fistula Risk Score, margin strategy including the 1 mm R0 concept, vascular resection indications, artery-first approach rationale, and pylorus preservation.

**Key numbers and benchmarks introduced here:**  
53.5 months vs 35.5 months median OS (PRODIGE 24); 43.2% vs 31.4% five-year OS (PRODIGE 24); vein-abutment ≤180 degrees reclassification; 16.3% vs 3.8% mortality (very-low vs very-high volume hospitals).

**Cross-file dependencies:**  
All citation numbers [1]–[33] that appear in this file are fully defined in `chunk_04_bibtex_refs_1_to_26.md` (refs 1–26) and `chunk_05_bibtex_refs_27_to_52.md` (refs 27–33). The executive summary presupposes content from Sections 5–15 found in chunks 02 and 03, so this file should be read alongside those files for complete context.

### chunk_02_sections_5_to_9.md

**What it contains:**  
Five consecutive numbered sections covering the clinical outcomes and procedural evidence base:

- **Section 5 — Patient success rates and survival after Whipple for pancreatic cancer:** Covers CONKO-001 (gemcitabine adjuvant, 5-year OS 20.7% vs 10.4%, 10-year OS 12.2% vs 7.7%), ESPAC-4 long-term data (median OS 31.6 vs 28.4 months overall; 49.9 vs 32.2 months in R0-resected patients), PRODIGE 24 five-year outcomes (53.5 vs 35.5 months median OS; 43.2% vs 31.4% five-year OS), and APACT (nab-paclitaxel plus gemcitabine). Defines the contemporary patient-success benchmark and the AI-trial endpoint minimum set.
- **Section 6 — Death rates and preventable mortality pathways:** Historical to modern mortality trajectory, volume-outcome reproducibility (Fischer 2023 systematic review), the complication-cascade death pathway (fistula → abscess → pseudoaneurysm → hemorrhage → organ failure), and the 2026 ISGPS postpancreatectomy mortality framework as the required evaluation lens for AI systems.
- **Section 7 — Morbidity: pancreatic fistula, hemorrhage, delayed gastric emptying, and failure to rescue:** The 2016/2017 ISGPS fistula redefinition (biochemical leak concept; grade B/C = clinically relevant), 17% fistula incidence (Zhang 2022 meta-analysis), Fistula Risk Score (Callery 2013) and alternative FRS (Mungroop 2019), postpancreatectomy hemorrhage ISGPS definition (Wente 2007), delayed gastric emptying ISGPS definition and 27.7%/14.3% rates (Wente 2007), early drain removal evidence (Dai 2022; Ricci 2024), pancreatic duct stenting evidence (Dai 2025), and pasireotide trial (Allen 2014).
- **Section 8 — Perioperative care breakthroughs:** ERAS 2019 guidelines (Melloul 2020) and updated cost-benefit evidence (Noba 2023), preoperative optimization checklist, selective biliary drainage rationale, blood management, and the model of the strongest safety program.
- **Section 9 — Open, laparoscopic, and robotic Whipple evidence:** Historical timeline from Gagner-Pomp 1994 through LEOPARD-2, PADULAP, Qin 2024 Chinese RCT 3-year follow-up, Ricci 2025 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs (1428 patients; 2.9% vs 2.6% mortality), Giulianotti 2003 robotic debut, Liu 2024 Chinese RCT (11.0 vs 13.5 days LOS), EUROPA 2024 Heidelberg RCT, Tang 2024 meta-analysis (2 RCTs + 22 propensity-matched studies), and Emmen 2025 Dutch 1000-case cohort (10.1% conversion; 41.3% grade III+ complications; 24.4% grade B/C fistula; 3.9% mortality; 47% ideal outcome rate).

**Key numbers and benchmarks introduced here:**  
CONKO-001 survival data; ESPAC-4 long-term survival by R-status; PRODIGE 24 five-year OS; 17% fistula incidence; 27.7% DGE average; Dutch robotic cohort key metrics; 2.9% vs 2.6% mortality across minimally invasive RCT meta-analysis.

**Cross-file dependencies:**  
Builds directly on the definitions established in chunk 01 (fistula risk, margin definitions, procedure scope). Citation numbers [1]–[52] appear; all are defined in chunks 04 and 05. The survival benchmarks in Section 5 are the oncologic standard against which AI-trial endpoints in chunk 03 (Section 12) are calibrated. The ISGPS complication definitions in Section 7 are the foundation for the data-collection schema in chunk 03 (Section 12). The Dutch 1000-case cohort data in Section 9 is the primary robotic safety baseline referenced throughout chunk 03.

### chunk_03_sections_10_to_15.md

**What it contains:**  
The concluding six numbered sections, which together form the forward-looking AI-trial specification:

- **Section 10 — Learning curve and credentialing baseline:** Dutch four-phase learning curve cutoffs (1–15, 16–62, 63–84, >84 cases), improvements across phases (operative time, conversion, DGE, reoperation, LOS) versus non-improvements (major morbidity, mortality, ideal outcome), and the minimum credentialing requirements for a baseline AI-assisted robotic program.
- **Section 11 — Breakthroughs that matter for future AI-assisted robotic Whipple:** Computer vision capabilities (phase recognition, anatomic segmentation, vessel proximity, force estimation, bleeding detection, skill assessment), SAGES video governance consensus (Eckhoff 2023; Meireles 2021), OR data recording white paper (Schnelldorfer 2024), FDA Good Machine Learning Practice principles (FDAGMLP2025), FDA 2025 PCCP final guidance (FDAPCCP2025), STAR autonomous suturing (Shademan 2016) and Saeidi 2022 laparoscopic anastomosis as complexity benchmarks, and the near-term AI mandate of assistance not autonomy.
- **Section 12 — Baseline dataset and metrics for AI-assisted robotic Whipple programs:** Complete four-domain data dictionary — preoperative variables (25+), intraoperative variables (20+), postoperative variables (18+), oncologic variables (15+), and human factors variables (10+). Primary safety endpoints (90-day mortality, grade C fistula, grade C hemorrhage, unplanned conversion, major morbidity, failure to rescue). Secondary endpoints (R0, LN yield, blood loss, transfusion, operative time, LOS, readmission, reoperation, time to adjuvant therapy, adjuvant completion, patient-reported recovery, cost, staff workload). Algorithmic fairness mandate.
- **Section 13 — Current baseline conclusions as of May 12, 2026:** Eight summary statements covering mortality reduction drivers, survival improvement drivers, the neoadjuvant sequencing uncertainty, the fistula challenge, the robotic opportunity (assistance not autonomy), the robotic diffusion risk, and the appropriate AI baseline definition.
- **Section 14 — Practical baseline thresholds for future AI-assisted robotic studies:** Six minimum packages — comparator standard, stratification variables, safety reporting period, oncologic follow-up minimum, AI validation package, video-data package, and implementation package including stopping rules.
- **Section 15 — Bottom line:** Five closing statements integrating safety, systemic therapy, robotics maturity, AI development principles, and the definition of the strongest baseline.

**Key frameworks introduced here:**  
The four-domain registry data dictionary; primary and secondary AI-trial endpoint sets; the six minimum package requirements; the eight-statement baseline conclusion set; the near-term AI mandate (assistance not autonomy).

**Cross-file dependencies:**  
This file is the operational synthesis of the entire evidence base. It draws on survival benchmarks from chunk 02 (Section 5), complication definitions from chunk 02 (Sections 7–8), robotic safety data from chunk 02 (Section 9), learning curve data from chunk 02 (Section 9) and Section 10 of this file, and regulatory frameworks cited here but defined in chunks 04 and 05. The data dictionary in Section 12 references variables introduced across chunks 01 and 02. The stopping rules in Section 14 reference the ISGPS complication grading from chunk 02.

## How all five files relate to each other

The five files form a single layered evidence structure:

**Layer 1 — Definitional and historical foundation (chunk 01):** Establishes what the Whipple procedure is, who it is for, how it evolved, what resectability means in 2026, and what the operative goals are. All subsequent files assume these definitions.

**Layer 2 — Clinical outcomes and procedural evidence (chunk 02):** Populates the evidence base with quantitative survival data, complication incidence data, perioperative care evidence, and the complete comparative effectiveness record for open, laparoscopic, and robotic approaches. The survival numbers here set the oncologic bar; the complication definitions here set the safety language; the robotic RCT and registry data here set the current-practice baseline.

**Layer 3 — AI-trial specification and synthesis (chunk 03):** Translates the evidence in layers 1 and 2 into a concrete program design: learning curve thresholds, technology requirements, a four-domain data dictionary, primary and secondary endpoints, minimum validation packages, stopping rules, and a final evidence synthesis. This file is the direct input for trial protocol drafting.

**Layer 4 — Reference resolution, first half (chunk 04):** Provides the full bibliographic data for all citations [1]–[26]. Every factual claim in chunks 01–03 that carries a bracketed citation number in the range [1]–[26] resolves here.

**Layer 5 — Reference resolution, second half (chunk 05):** Provides the full bibliographic data for citations [27]–[52]. Every bracketed citation number in the range [27]–[52] in chunks 01–03 resolves here. This file also contains the terminal line "Full report with BibTeX entries last," which is the closing line of the original source document.

## Instructions for Claude Code Opus 4.7 processing this file set

1. Ingest all five chunk files and this README in a single context window. The full set is approximately 25,000–30,000 tokens and fits comfortably within the 1M context limit.

2. The three text chunks (01–03) together contain the complete paper body. No content has been omitted, reordered, or paraphrased. When drafting a new trial paper, treat this text as the authoritative evidence baseline.

3. When resolving a citation number from the body text, look up the BibTeX key in chunk 04 (refs [1]–[26]) or chunk 05 (refs [27]–[52]) by matching the bracketed number to its position in the citation-order sequence described in the README tables above. The BibTeX key, DOI, and URL fields are all present for every entry.

4. The Section 12 data dictionary in chunk 03 is the most operationally complete input for designing a new trial's CRF (case report form), registry schema, or data management plan. It is organized into five domains: preoperative, intraoperative, postoperative, oncologic, and human factors.

5. The primary and secondary endpoint sets in chunk 03 Section 12 are directly derived from the evidence in chunks 01–02. When designing statistical analysis plans, map each endpoint back to its evidentiary source: 90-day mortality to ISGPS 2026 [20] (chunk 04), fistula grading to Bassi 2017 [17] (chunk 04), hemorrhage grading to Wente 2007 [18] (chunk 04), DGE grading to Wente 2007 [19] (chunk 04), R0 definition to Tummers 2019 and Leonhardt 2022 [31,32] (chunk 05), learning curve phases to Emmen 2025 [44] and Zwart 2023 [45] (chunk 05), AI regulatory standards to FDA PCCP 2025 [46] and FDA GMLP 2025 [47] (chunk 05), and video governance to Eckhoff 2023 [48], Meireles 2021 [49], and Schnelldorfer 2024 [50] (chunk 05).

6. The minimum package requirements in chunk 03 Section 14 (comparator, stratification, safety reporting, oncologic follow-up, AI validation, video data, implementation) are designed to prevent the most common failure modes of AI-surgical trials. Each package item has a corresponding reference cluster in chunks 04 and 05.

7. When drafting background or rationale sections for the new trial paper, the executive summary in chunk 01 provides a compressed, citation-backed narrative that can serve as a starting template.

8. Algorithmic fairness (chunk 03 Section 12, final paragraph) is a required design element, not optional. Training data diversity, demographic stratification, and deployment-population validation should be addressed explicitly in any new trial protocol derived from this baseline.
